# APEX INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY & HIGHER SCIENCES
## OFFICIAL UNIVERSITY ACADEMIC REGULATIONS & DEGREE ORDINANCE
**Document Reference:** AIT/ACAD/REG/2026-V3  
**Effective Academic Session:** 2025–2026 Onwards  
**Applicability:** All Undergraduate (B.Tech, B.S.), Postgraduate (M.Tech, M.S.), and Doctoral (Ph.D.) Degree Candidates

### PREAMBLE & INSTITUTIONAL CHARTER
The Apex Institute of Technology & Higher Sciences (hereinafter designated as the "Institute") establishes these comprehensive Academic Regulations to govern curricular architectures, matriculation standards, course registration guidelines, evaluation schemes, disciplinary codes, and the conferral of academic degrees. Every matriculated student is obligated to familiarize themselves thoroughly with every provision stipulated herein. Ignorance of any clause or administrative amendment published by the Academic Senate shall not be admissible as a defense in any academic, financial, or disciplinary proceeding.

---

### SECTION 1: MATRICULATION, REGISTRATION, AND ENROLLMENT FRAMEWORK

#### 1.1 Semester Registration Protocol
1. Every candidate admitted into any program offered by the Institute must complete formal registration at the commencement of each academic semester on the designated dates specified in the Official Academic Calendar published by the Office of the Dean of Academic Affairs.
2. Semester registration entails:
   - Settlement of all prescribed semester tuition fees, amenity levies, hostel charges, and institutional dues.
   - Formal course selection and verification signed by the appointed Departmental Faculty Advisor.
   - Biometric identity verification at the Central Student Service Center.
3. Late registration shall be permitted exclusively up to five (5) working days subsequent to the standard registration deadline upon payment of a non-waivable late registration fee of Rs. 3,500. Registration beyond this five-day grace period is strictly barred, resulting in mandatory semester deferral.

#### 1.2 Maximum and Minimum Credit Loads
1. In any standard regular semester (Autumn or Spring), a full-time undergraduate student must register for a minimum course workload of sixteen (16) academic credits and cannot exceed twenty-six (26) academic credits, excluding non-credit mandatory induction activities.
2. Under extraordinary conditions, an undergraduate student maintaining a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 8.50 or higher may petition the Departmental Undergraduate Committee (DUGC) to register for an additional elective course up to an absolute ceiling of thirty (30) credits.
3. Postgraduate students must enroll in a minimum of twelve (12) credits and a maximum of twenty (20) credits per regular semester.

#### 1.3 Course Prerequisites and Corequisites
1. Progression to advanced courses is contingent upon satisfying all prescribed foundational prerequisite courses with a minimum passing grade of 'D' (or 'P' in Pass/No-Credit courses).
2. A student who has received an 'F' (Fail) or 'I' (Incomplete) grade in a mandatory prerequisite course is categorically prohibited from registering for the subsequent dependent course until the prerequisite has been cleared in a supplementary examination or regular re-registration.

### SECTION 2: CURRICULAR STRUCTURE AND DEGREE COMPLETION REQUIREMENTS

#### 2.1 Credit Distribution Model for Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech)
To qualify for the conferment of the Bachelor of Technology degree, a matriculated candidate must satisfactorily earn a minimum aggregate of one hundred and sixty (160) earned academic credits distributed across the following pedagogical verticals:
- Basic Sciences Vertical (Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology): 24 Credits
- Engineering Sciences Vertical (Computing, Mechanical Workshop, Electronics, Graphics): 22 Credits
- Humanities, Social Sciences, Management, and Professional Ethics (HSM): 14 Credits
- Departmental Core Vertical (Discipline-specific Theory and Laboratory Suites): 58 Credits
- Professional Discipline Electives: 18 Credits
- Open / Institute-Wide Interdisciplinary Electives: 12 Credits
- Capstone Senior Design Project (Parts I & II): 12 Credits
- Mandatory Non-Credit Audit Courses (Constitution of India, Environmental Sciences): Satisfactory Completion

#### 2.2 Maximum Duration for Degree Completion
1. The standard duration for the completion of the B.Tech program is eight (8) consecutive academic semesters (four calendar years).
2. The maximum allowable time ceiling permitted for an undergraduate student to fulfill all curricular requirements for the award of the B.Tech degree is twelve (12) consecutive semesters (six calendar years) from the initial date of matriculation. Any student failing to complete all credit requirements within this maximum duration shall automatically forfeit their student status and be permanently deregistered.
3. For M.Tech candidates, the normal duration is four (4) semesters, with an absolute completion ceiling of six (6) semesters (three calendar years).

### SECTION 3: GRADING SCHEME, EVALUATION METRICS, AND HONORS CRITERIA

#### 3.1 Absolute and Relative Letter Grading Scale
The Institute adopts a 10-point letter grading system based on continuous internal assessments and summative end-semester examinations. The corresponding grade points and descriptive performance indices are tabulated below:

| Letter Grade | Performance Descriptor | Grade Points Assigned | Qualitative Assessment Index |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A+** | Outstanding | 10.0 | Extraordinary mastery and exceptional analytical depth |
| **A** | Excellent | 9.0 | Comprehensive conceptual understanding and execution |
| **B+** | Very Good | 8.0 | High standard of subject competency and problem-solving |
| **B** | Good | 7.0 | Above average grasp of core curricular concepts |
| **C+** | Fair | 6.0 | Average subject proficiency meeting basic expectations |
| **C** | Satisfactory | 5.0 | Minimum acceptable performance standard |
| **D** | Marginal Passing | 4.0 | Bare threshold passing standard; remediation advised |
| **F** | Failing Grade | 0.0 | Unsatisfactory mastery; mandatory course repeat required |
| **I** | Incomplete Grade | 0.0 | Granted only for verified institutional absence |
| **W** | Formally Withdrawn | — | Recorded upon sanctioned administrative withdrawal |

#### 3.2 Computation of SGPA and CGPA
1. **Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA)** is calculated at the end of each semester as:
   \[ \text{SGPA} = \frac{\sum (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum C_i} \]
   where \(C_i\) denotes the credit weight of the \(i\)-th course and \(G_i\) is the numerical grade point secured.
2. **Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA)** is the weighted cumulative measure across all completed semesters:
   \[ \text{CGPA} = \frac{\sum_{\text{all}} (C_k \times G_k)}{\sum_{\text{all}} C_k} \]

#### 3.3 Dean’s Honors List and Merit Distinctions
1. An undergraduate student who secures an SGPA of 9.20 or above in any regular semester, while carrying at least twenty (20) registered credits without any 'I' or 'F' grades, shall be cited on the Dean's Academic Honors Roll.
2. A student graduating with a final CGPA of 8.50 or higher, who has completed the curriculum within the nominal eight semesters without any academic backlogs or disciplinary sanctions, shall have the honor "Graduated with Distinction" inscribed upon their final degree scroll.

### SECTION 4: CLASSROOM ATTENDANCE REGULATIONS AND EXAM ELIGIBILITY

#### 4.1 Philosophy of Academic Engagement
Regular physical attendance in all lectures, tutorials, laboratory experiments, and seminars is an indispensable tenet of the Institute’s instructional standard. Faculty members are mandated to record attendance through the automated biometric enterprise portal at the commencement of each instructional contact session.

#### 4.2 Mandatory Attendance Standard [CRITICAL INSTRUCTIONAL POLICY]
A student must maintain a minimum of 75% attendance in each registered course to be eligible to appear for the end-semester examination. Under no circumstances whatsoever shall any condonation or relaxation be granted below this 75% absolute threshold, regardless of medical emergencies, official representations, or administrative discretion. Any candidate accumulating less than 75% physical presence in lectures, tutorials, and practical laboratories shall be automatically awarded an 'F' (Attendance Shortage Fail) grade, barred from the examination hall, and compelled to repeat the course in its entirety during the subsequent summer term or regular academic session.

#### 4.3 Automated Biometric Attendance Audit
Attendance tallies are processed weekly by the Central Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) server. Warning electronic notifications shall be automatically dispatched to the student's institutional email address and parent portal whenever aggregate course attendance drops below 80%.

### SECTION 5: EXAMINATION PROCEDURES, MID-SEMESTER AND END-SEMESTER CONDUCT

#### 5.1 Continuous Assessment Weightage Breakdown
Evaluation in all lecture-based courses is partitioned as follows:
- Continuous Class Assessments (surprise quizzes, weekly homework sets, term papers): 20%
- Mid-Semester Examination (administered centrally in the 8th instructional week): 30%
- Laboratory Performance and Practical Viva Voce (where applicable): 15%
- Comprehensive End-Semester Examination (conducted over two weeks at semester conclusion): 50% (or 35% in courses paired with dedicated laboratory components).

#### 5.2 Examination Hall Protocols and Security
1. Candidates must arrive at the examination venue twenty (20) minutes prior to the scheduled paper commencement. No candidate shall be permitted into the exam hall fifteen (15) minutes after the bell rings.
2. Every examinee must place their official laminated Institute Identity Card on the desk throughout the testing window.
3. Possession of mobile phones, smartwatches, programmable calculators, Bluetooth earphones, handwritten crib sheets, or unauthorized electronic memory devices inside the examination hall constitutes gross academic malpractice under Section 8.

### SECTION 6: COURSE ADD/DROP PROCEDURES, AUDIT REGISTRATION, AND WITHDRAWALS

#### 6.1 Course Add Period
A student may add an elective course or switch tutorial sections within five (5) instructional days from the beginning of the semester, provided physical seating capacity is available and written approval is granted by the Course Instructor and Faculty Advisor.

#### 6.2 Audit Course Option
1. An undergraduate student in their sixth semester or higher with a CGPA exceeding 7.50 may register for up to one course per semester as an 'Audit' course.
2. An audited course does not compute toward SGPA or CGPA calculations; however, the student must satisfy at least 75% attendance and complete all internal mid-term examinations to earn an 'AU' notation on the grade sheet.

#### 6.3 Formal Course Withdrawal
A student encountering severe difficulty may formally petition to withdraw from a single course before the close of the sixth instructional week. The transcript will display a grade notation of 'W', which carries no numerical penalty but incurs no credit towards graduation.

#### 6.4 Course Drop Deadlines and Financial Liability [FINANCIAL FORFEITURE CLAUSE]
A student may drop a course without academic penalty up to Day 7 of instructional classes. If a course is dropped or a student withdraws from any course after Day 7 of the semester, the student incurs full financial liability and 100% fee forfeiture; absolutely zero refund of tuition or lab fees shall be sanctioned. The institute will neither re-credit nor carry over any portion of the assessed course tuition fee toward future academic semesters under any circumstances.

### SECTION 7: SUPPLEMENTARY, RE-EXAMINATIONS, AND GRADE IMPROVEMENT

#### 7.1 Summer Remedial Term
1. The Institute conducts an intensive seven-week Summer Remedial Term during June and July for students who have obtained an 'F' grade in mandatory departmental core courses during the preceding academic year.
2. A student may register for a maximum of two (2) courses during the Summer Term upon remittance of the prescribed summer remedial tuition fee of Rs. 8,000 per course.
3. The highest grade attainable in any course completed through the Summer Remedial Term is capped at 'B+' (8.0 Grade Points).

#### 7.2 Grade Improvement Examination
An undergraduate student who has passed all courses but possesses a cumulative CGPA below 6.50 at the end of their seventh semester may apply for a Grade Improvement Examination in up to two theoretical subjects. The better of the two grades obtained shall be retained on the official transcript.

### SECTION 8: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY, MALPRACTICE, AND ETHICAL STANDARDS

#### 8.1 Plagiarism and Code Copying
1. The Institute enforces rigorous standards regarding original intellectual synthesis. Any project report, dissertation, literature survey, or software programming assignment submitted for evaluation must undergo automated screening via institutional plagiarism verification software.
2. A textual or algorithmic similarity index exceeding fifteen percent (15%) excluding standard bibliographic citations shall result in immediate assignment rejection and an automatic 'Zero' score for that component.
3. Submitting machine-generated text or uncredited code authored by AI models without explicit declaration in project methodology documentation constitutes academic dishonesty.

#### 8.2 Malpractice Disciplinary Committee (MDC)
Any student apprehended copying in examinations, possessing illicit reference sheets, assisting peers through whispering or hand signals, or transmitting digital messages shall be summoned before the standing MDC. Penalties imposed by the MDC include:
- Category 1 (Minor infraction): Cancellation of the specific examination paper and award of 'F' grade.
- Category 2 (Deliberate cheating or electronic aid): Cancellation of all examinations registered in that semester and mandatory suspension for one academic term.
- Category 3 (Impersonation, organized leakage, or assault of invigilator): Permanent expulsion from the Institute and referral to civic law enforcement authorities.

### SECTION 9: SPECIAL CONDONATIONS, DISABILITY ACCOMMODATIONS, AND EXTENUATING LEAVE

#### 9.1 Medical & Extenuating Circumstances Condonation [ATTENDANCE RELAXATION PROVISION]
Students who fail to meet the standard attendance criterion due to certified hospitalization or prolonged illness verified by the Chief Medical Officer may be granted condonation of up to 10%, permitting them to sit for examinations with an attendance threshold of not less than 65%. To be eligible for this condonation, the student or their authorized guardian must file the official medical certificate along with discharge summaries at the Academic Dean’s office within seven (7) calendar days of discharge from the medical facility.

#### 9.2 Official Sports, Cultural, and Technical Delegations
Students selected to represent the Institute or the nation in official inter-university sports tournaments, national youth festivals, or international Olympiads are entitled to duty leave. The authorized days of absence shall be credited toward physical attendance, provided prior written clearance is sanctioned by the Dean of Student Affairs.

#### 9.3 Physical Disability Accommodations
Candidates with registered physical, visual, or neurological disabilities certified by an accredited state medical authority are entitled to compensatory extra time of twenty (20) minutes per hour of examination and the provision of an approved non-technical scribe.

### SECTION 10: ACADEMIC PROBATION, WARNING, AND DISMISSAL

#### 10.1 Academic Warning
A student who secures an SGPA below 5.00 in any given semester shall receive an official Academic Warning Letter issued to their registered guardian. The student is required to consult their Departmental Faculty Advisor weekly.

#### 10.2 Academic Probation Criteria
A student whose Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) falls below 4.50 at the conclusion of any academic year shall be placed on formal Academic Probation for the subsequent semester. A student on Academic Probation:
- Is strictly barred from participating in inter-collegiate competitions, sports squads, or campus cultural events.
- Cannot contest any student club or society office-bearer position.
- Workload is restricted to a maximum of sixteen (16) credits to facilitate academic recovery.

#### 10.3 Termination of Candidature
A student who remains on Academic Probation for two consecutive semesters and fails to raise their CGPA to at least 4.50 shall have their program candidature permanently terminated.

### SECTION 11: SENIOR CAPSTONE, THESIS GUIDELINES, AND RESEARCH LAB ACCESS

#### 11.1 Senior Capstone Project Administration
All undergraduate candidates in the seventh and eighth semesters must execute a senior capstone design project under the direct supervision of an allocated faculty guide. The capstone evaluates problem formulation, experimental implementation, engineering rigor, and communication skills.

#### 11.2 Senior Capstone Research Lab Access Protocol [LAB ACCESS GUARANTEE]
All registered final-year undergraduate and postgraduate students working on their capstone thesis or funded departmental research projects are guaranteed continuous 24-hour, seven-days-a-week (24/7) unrestricted physical and biometric access to academic department research laboratories and computational clusters. Departmental security personnel and facilities administrators are explicitly prohibited from barring or interrupting legitimate project researchers engaged in laboratory experimentation during night or weekend hours.

### SECTION 12: DEGREE CONFERMENT, CONVOCATION, AND COMMENCEMENT

#### 12.1 Eligibility for Degree Conferral
A candidate shall be declared eligible for the award of the B.Tech degree only upon satisfying all of the following requirements:
1. Accumulation of the mandatory 160 earned academic credits across all specified verticals.
2. Attainment of a final CGPA of not less than 5.00.
3. No pending 'F' or 'I' grades in any core, elective, or non-credit mandatory requirement.
4. Full clearance of all outstanding tuition, library fines, hostel dues, and laboratory breakage dues authenticated by a signed Institutional 'No Dues Certificate'.
5. No pending disciplinary, malpractice, or anti-ragging penalties recorded against their institutional dossier.

#### 12.2 Issuance of Provisional Degree and Migration Certificates
Eligible graduates may obtain their Provisional Degree Certificate and Transcript within fourteen (14) working days after the official declaration of final semester results, pending formal presentation during the Annual Convocation Ceremony.

### SECTION 13: GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL, RE-EVALUATION, AND ARBITRATION

#### 13.1 Inspection of Answer Scripts
A student dissatisfied with their evaluation in an end-semester theory examination may submit an application for script viewing within five (5) working days from the result announcement, along with an administrative processing fee of Rs. 500 per course. The student shall inspect their evaluated answer booklet in the presence of the Course Instructor.

#### 13.2 Formal Re-Evaluation Procedure
If a computational omission or discrepancy is detected during script inspection, the student may petition for formal independent re-evaluation upon payment of Rs. 1,500. The script shall be evaluated blindly by an external examiner appointed by the Controller of Examinations. The revised mark shall replace the original mark, whether higher or lower.

### SECTION 14: STATUTORY AUTHORITY, AMENDMENTS, AND EXTRAORDINARY POWERS

#### 14.1 Academic Senate Supremacy
The Academic Senate is the supreme legislative body of the Institute on all academic matters. The Senate reserves the absolute prerogative to alter, amend, repeal, or introduce new clauses to these regulations at its discretion.

#### 14.2 Departmental Academic Advisory Committee
The DAAC assists in curriculum oversight, reviewing course syllabi, approving elective offerings, and evaluating student petitions concerning minor scheduling anomalies.

#### 14.3 Extraordinary Dean's Discretionary Powers [UNCONDITIONAL WAIVER AUTHORITY]
Notwithstanding any general regulation or specific clause contained herein, the Dean of Academic Affairs possesses exclusive, final, and unconditional authority to waive attendance deficiencies down to a minimum of 50% for students demonstrating exceptional merit, institutional service, or compassionate grounds. Any written determination or executive decree rendered under this power by the Dean of Academic Affairs shall be binding, non-appealable, and shall supersede all other conflicting provisions, faculty rulings, and departmental determinations throughout the Institute.

### SECTION 15: INTERNATIONAL STUDENT EXCHANGE AND DUAL DEGREE PATHWAYS

#### 15.1 Eligibility for Semester Abroad Programs
1. Undergraduate students who have completed their fourth semester with a CGPA not lower than 8.00, with no standing academic backlogs or disciplinary records, are eligible to petition for an approved International Semester Abroad Program at a partner university.
2. The student must submit an official Course Equivalence Matrix pre-approved by the Departmental Undergraduate Committee (DUGC) and endorsed by the Dean of Academic Affairs prior to departure.
3. Upon return, the student must provide official certified transcripts issued by the partner institution. Transcripts will be translated into letter grades in accordance with the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) conversion rubric authorized by the Academic Senate.

#### 15.2 Financial Obligations During International Study
1. Tuition fee remittance during the study-abroad semester is governed by bilateral Memoranda of Understanding (MoU). Where reciprocal tuition waivers exist, candidates pay standard tuition to Apex Institute.
2. In all other arrangements, travel, visas, foreign lodging, health insurance, and local municipal registrations are the sole individual liability of the candidate.

### SECTION 16: INDUSTRIAL INTERNSHIPS, STARTUP INCUBATION, AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

#### 16.1 Structured Industrial Internship Credits
1. All undergraduate engineering students are required to undergo a mandatory eight-week industrial internship during the summer recess following their sixth semester.
2. Satisfactory completion of the internship, authenticated by a designated corporate mentor evaluation report and a formal technical presentation before the departmental evaluation committee, confers four (4) mandatory curricular credits.
3. Students pursuing off-campus internships during regular instructional semesters must secure prior written sanction from the Head of Department and the Institute Training and Placement Officer (TPO).

#### 16.2 Technology Transfer and Institutional Inventions
1. Any patentable invention, design registration, or intellectual property developed utilizing institutional laboratories, computing resources, or financial seed grants from the Institute remains the joint intellectual property of the student inventors and the Apex Institute.
2. Commercial royalties generated from authorized licensing shall be apportioned under a sixty-forty (60:40) ratio in favor of the primary inventors, following deduction of administrative filing expenses.
